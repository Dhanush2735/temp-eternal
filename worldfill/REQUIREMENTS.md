## How to read this list

- **Done:** built and tested here.
- **Partly:** some of it works; what is left is written next to it.
- **Not yet:** nothing works yet.
- **In Terra++:** your Terra++ build (`terra3/`) has it, according to its own notes. I have read them but not checked
  it in a game.
- **Recorded / estimated / projected (revised 2026-10-24):** two of these were always there. Up to today an answer is
  *recorded* (a source says it) or *estimated* (no source, believable, marked in the data). The range now runs to
  3000 AD, and for the years after today there is nothing to record, so a third mark is added: *projected* — one
  believable continuation, generated by rules from the world's own trends, marked in the data as projected. The
  player is told an answer is projected only where the project already tells them an answer is estimated.
- **What "each and every" means here:** everything history records with a date and a place, from the sources

  estimates in the data (your turn-6 rule). Nothing that nobody recorded can be copied exactly.
- **Who does what:**
  - Terra++ builds the land, buildings and roads, and changes them with the year, the timeline and the universe.
  - The mod (Eternal Engine) does history, people, money, goods and the stories, and tells Terra++ what changed.

## 1. Earth 1:1 with buildings and roads, 3000 BC to 3000 AD

**"1:1" is not reachable as worded, and this line is here so the requirement says so.** The project's own
measurements: 1 km is 1,036 blocks at Hyderabad and 1,294 at Beijing (the scale varies by place); true north
is turned 29° from Minecraft north at Hyderabad; heights above 1,000 m and depths below 2,000 m are squashed
(Everest is 1,969 blocks). The reachable standard is **near 1:1 with the distortion measured, documented and
consistent enough to navigate by** — 435 places already convert to a block and back within 1 m.

**Done when:** in each test place and year, no building or road stands before its time or after its end, the famous
buildings of that year stand, and a town of the past looks like a town of its time and size.
Test places: Hyderabad in 1500, 1700, 1948 and 2026; Delhi 1650; Rome AD 100; London 1700; Beijing 1500;
Cusco 1500; Ur 2500 BC; Mohenjo-daro 2500 BC.

**With the far end moved to 3000 AD (2026-10-24), the same rule is applied forward:** in 2100, 2400 and 3000 the
land may not show a building that the projection has not brought into being by then, and every future change of a
place must be marked *projected* in the data. The 3000 AD end is not a recorded state — it is one continuation, and
the test is that it is internally consistent (a city's size, its water, its roads, its population and its money agree
with each other and with the years before them), not that it is true.

## 2. Every history from 3000 BC to 3000 AD

**Done when:** for any place and year the game says who ruled, what happened nearby and when, how many people lived
there, and what they spoke, believed, made and paid with. In a spot check of 200 random answers, fewer than 1 in 50
is wrong.

**Up to today** the spot check is against sources: *recorded* answers must match, *estimated* ones must be believable.
**After today (2026 → 3000 AD, revised 2026-10-24)** there is nothing to check against, so the standard changes to four
rules, and each is testable without a source:

1. **It continues, it does not restart.** 2027 follows 2026 — rulers, borders, money, technology and populations move
   by plausible steps, none of them jumping a century.
2. **It is one future, not a bag of options.** Every place shares the same projected world; a projected border in
   2400 agrees with the projected state next to it.
3. **It is marked.** Every projected answer says *projected*, and the player is told so.
4. **The player can overwrite it.** A changed history (R5) or a broken paradox (R6) replaces the projection for the
   places and years it touches — the projection is the default road, not a cage.

## How the risk of testing only at the end is kept small

- Every step adds its own lines to `/ee check all`, so the final test checks everything in one command.
- Every new system has an on/off setting, so a part that misbehaves in your final test can be switched off without
  losing the rest.
- The README records each step's tests, and `TEST_CHECKLIST.md` gets the final test's steps.
- Problems that only a real game shows (like the freeze and the people vanishing) will only appear at the final test.
  That is the cost of testing at the end, and step 39 is there for them.
