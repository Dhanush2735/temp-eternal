# 601–641 CE: partial research and game history updates

**Partly worked:** 601, 611, 621, 631 and 641 CE. The five year pages, initial sections of the corresponding decade pages and selected linked articles were checked. Many links remain unchecked. Decade pages reuse year-page content and are not independent confirmation. Wikipedia pages were current when read and generally not revision-pinned.

## Indexed game JSON

Installed **30 updates and two additions** across six indexed packs. Existing IDs were preserved. The additions are a 611 Antioch date-conflict note and a 621 Kaiyuan Tongbao coinage note. A name scan across nearby indexed years found no matching coinage entry; this does not establish semantic uniqueness. The Antioch note explicitly references a conflicting chronology and is not asserted to be a separate battle from the active 613 entry `q4870314`.

The before/after manifest is `reviews/601_641_ce_context_2026-10.json.gz`, canonical SHA-256 `97e73f794c681df4b413477ed1d46fc9feb37378e28f7b2754787a249a88d3d5`. `apply_601_641_ce_context.py` stages, applies or rolls back after checking exact before/after images. Writes are atomic per pack, not across all packs.

- **601:** Corrected Agilulf's northern-Italy reference from a point near Vienna. Peter's Tisza campaign is a regional note: the campaign article describes an advance in 601 but places earlier Tisza battles in 599. The Qieyun record now names compiler Lu Fayan and uses Chang'an as a contextual reference, not a proven publication site. The 601 Gregorian mission note avoids claiming all named missionaries and the *Libellus responsionum* reached Canterbury together. Padua's stored 601 siege slot is qualified because Agilulf's biography dates capture to 602.
- **611:** Corrected Cynegils's entry from treaty to succession and qualified his uncertain family relationships. Four Gates Pagoda's inscription supports its 611 construction date. The existing Caesarea record is specified as Caesarea in Cappadocia: the linked article describes a Byzantine blockade of Persian-held forces, not the separate coastal Caesarea. Antioch's 611 year-page claim conflicts with the linked 613 battle chronology; the new note does not create a second active battle.
- **621:** Málaga's conquest date is uncertain: the year-page claim is uncited and Suintila's biography attributes it to the earlier reign of Sisebut. Reccared II's reign length is debated and his claimed family connection and regency by Suintila are not established by the checked biographies. The 621 porcelain-bureau claim is now an uncertain cultural note. The Night Journey is identified as a religious tradition with uncertain dating. The new Kaiyuan coinage entry uses Chang'an as a capital proxy, not a verified mint location. The existing Hulao battle has a matching 621 battle article and was left active.
- **631:** Wogastisburg's site is unidentified and its date is 631 or 632; its previous reference point was in France. The Caracol war entry now describes the Maya star-war defeat of Naranjo, using Naranjo as a polity reference rather than a proven battlefield; linked accounts differ in how they emphasize Caracol and Calakmul. York's Anglian Tower has competing Roman, seventh-century and ninth-century datings. The Sasanian succession entries now distinguish Azarmidokht, Boran's restoration, and Hormizd VI's rival claim; Khosrow IV's linked biography instead dates his claim to 632–642. Corrected Sisenand, who is not identified as Suintila's son in their linked biographies, and moved his proclamation reference to Zaragoza. Moved Cyrus's reference from Turkey to Alexandria and qualified his 630/631 appointment date. Benjamin's precise refuge and date are not certain. Xuanzang's Hund point was moved from near Karachi to the northern Indus village; the village article itself has an LLM-content warning, so its historical assertions need independent checking.
- **641:** The Sebennytos battle is dated late 640 or early 641 by its article. Shushtar's article gives 641–642, but the stored boundary was not blindly extended. Genoa's capture date differs between 641 and around 643; the previous point near Vienna is now a Genoa reference note. Byzantine theme origins and attribution to Constans II remain debated; the 641 event is contextual rather than a definite reform. Moved Constans II's succession from Rhodes to Constantinople, and Uija's Baekje succession to a labeled Sabi capital reference. Clarified that Fustat is now part of Old Cairo, and corrected the misleading implication that Coptic culture ended with the Muslim conquest of Egypt.

Coordinates labeled as site, city, capital, village or region are gameplay references, not proof of exact event locations or geographic footprints. One-year slots can be uncertain markers. Different Java readers use different end-boundary conventions; this matters especially for the world and Wikidata packs. NewsIndex reads kinds independently of effects; a note is not guaranteed to disappear from news. War-pack records and saved-world behavior need their own reader-aware checks; do not assume a `note` field alone neutralizes every system.

## Unresolved and still active

- `q815105` is an active 641 Alexandria siege. The year page and linked siege article differ on dates and the subsequent military evacuation; no duration change was made. The broader conquest article places the campaign through 642. The precise relationship to other Egypt records has not been exhaustively reviewed.
- `hz_011345ba0b`, a 641 Harrat Rahat eruption hazard, remains active and unreviewed. Other 600s–640s decade-bucket hazards were not comprehensively audited. `Disasters.java` loads hazard rows independently; changing a hazard's effect or kind does not safely neutralize it.
- `wpr_0bc7575a5a` asserts a 641 Oswiu conquest of Gododdin; the year page calls the date approximate, and the biography does not directly establish this campaign. It remains active pending further source and location research. The Avar–Heraclius 621 treaty `wpr_7d92fc3712` retains awkward year-page wording and broad spatial effects. Tang/Western Turkic 641 entry `wpr_863c8c49d7` retains a broad China point despite an asserted Central Asian civil war and needs further source checking.
- Other records, semantic duplicates, conflicting dates and linked articles remain unchecked. Existing earlier unresolved earthquake, plague, integrity and disaster-count issues are unchanged. The 611–612 Caesarea article says it relies on a single source; the battle of Antioch article includes sourcing warnings.
