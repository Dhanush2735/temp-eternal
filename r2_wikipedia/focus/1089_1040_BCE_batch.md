# 1089–1040 BCE: partial R2 collection

**Progress: 2,268 years partly worked; 0 fully collected.** All 50 intervening year titles were checked and redirected to five decade articles. Only selected linked articles were researched. Five ten-year endpoints were already counted, so this batch adds 45 to the previous total of 2,223, under the existing broad shared-period convention. A redirected title is not an independent annual history, and the count does not mean these years are complete.

## Indexed game history

Three existing records were updated and one context note was added to indexed game resources:

- **Ramesses XI and Smendes:** changed the inherited political/treaty trigger to a note. The dates and details of the succession are disputed; 1077 BCE is a reference slot, and the map point does not establish where a death or accession took place. Several previously reviewed Egyptian transition notes overlap and remain for later deduplication.
- **Five planets, 1059 BCE:** changed a political trigger to an astronomical context note. A five-planet grouping has been reconstructed, but its identification with the transmitted *Bamboo Annals* account and its historical interpretation are debated. The inherited Chinese map point is not an independently verified observation site. This is not proof of a political effect.
- **Battle of Muye:** changed the Wikidata-derived exact-year military-presence trigger into a note while retaining its stable ID. The Shang–Zhou conquest is a historical transition, but 1046 BCE is an approximate reconstruction and details of the battle were shaped by later accounts. The inherited Xinxiang-area point is not independently verified as the battlefield. A related broader transition note remains at 1050 BCE; the two are not silently merged.
- **Codrus:** added a note for the Athenian tradition of his death and Medon's succession. The story and its 1068 BCE date are not independently established. Codrus's son Acastus is not automatically the similarly named Argonaut of Iolcus.

Earlier endpoint work had already qualified other entries, including incompatible 1079 BCE and later biographies of King Cheng of Zhou, the Three Guards rebellion, and several volcanic records. An inclusive scan found no stored intervals overlapping this range in the indexed hazard pack; it cannot prove that no disasters occurred or that other records are complete. One-year slots and inherited map points remain practical references, not evidence of exact dates or locations.
