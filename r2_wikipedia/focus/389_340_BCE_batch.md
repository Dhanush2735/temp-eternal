# 389–340 BCE: preliminary R2 Wikipedia pass

**Status: partly worked, not fully collected.** All 50 individual year titles resolved without redirects or missing pages; their revision wikitext is saved in `year_wikitext_389_340.json.gz` and title metadata in `year_titles_389_340.json`. Transclusions were not expanded. Five decade pages and selected linked articles were partly reviewed, not all linked pages or references. A scan found 235 indexed rows whose start falls in the interval; most were not individually reviewed, and rows starting earlier are outside that scan. This is not a complete worldwide history.

## Indexed gameplay improvements

Twenty guarded updates across three indexed packs (`reviews/389_340_bce_context_2026-10.json.gz`; canonical SHA-256 `64b32563643449dae87b74032ac28387ca14654a7bc48acd0ab227b8996c3efb`):

- Corrected imported battle or siege starts to the linked articles' years for Cynoscephalae (364), the Egyptian–Persian Pelusium/Mendes campaign (373), Lilybaeum (368) and Olynthus's fall (348 BCE). Stable IDs and military effects remain; exact footprints and ancient narrative details are not independently established. The world-pack NewsIndex treats the end year as inclusive while the gazetteer uses an exclusive end, potentially showing an extra year.
- Replaced Tegyra's 374 military trigger with a qualified 375/376 context: the article's infobox and prelude disagree. Qualified the sack of Rome as traditionally 390 or around 387–386 BCE, and the siege of Syracuse as 344–343/342, rather than asserting the inherited single-year triggers.
- Three Roman–Samnite battle rows are now contextual notes under the traditional 343 BCE convention. Their linked articles explain that the Varronian chronology may be four years too early; Livy's details and, in the Suessula article, the event's historicity have been questioned. This does not prove the battles did not occur. The First Samnite War, Third Sacred War, Social War and Theban–Spartan War are also regional notes rather than continuous military presence inside a single modeled circle. Individual separately indexed battle rows remain where applicable.
- For Helike, corrected the duplicate world row from 372 to 373 BCE and converted both the world lockdown and annual disruption rows to qualified notes; the latter's location was moved from Boeotia to an approximate Helike settlement point. Ancient accounts describe an earthquake and inundation; archaeological interpretations disagree about the mechanism, tsunami evidence and extent of destruction. Delphi temple damage was also made a note: one Greek culture source dates earthquake destruction to 373, whereas a 2022 study notes that identifying the cause with the Helike earthquake is an inference. These records are not evidence for independent disasters.
- Qualified the Guiling campaign because Handan was only one of several locations in the account and its exact battlefield timing/location needs review. The inherited statement that Zhao founded Handan in 386 was made a note, since the linked city article describes much earlier settlement; the political reference needs verification. Crocus Field is a 353-or-352 BCE note, not a certain one-year trigger.

One cross-pack hazard move (`reviews/389_340_bce_hazards_2026-10.json`; canonical SHA-256 `d9cf70388146eca20b032762af6358b5891f43152d373527cba1e4782a85637e`): NOAA earthquake event 21 cites a Roman Forum chasm and suggests an earthquake, but the interpretation and dating are uncertain, while the catalog's 35°N, 25°E coordinates are near Crete despite its Rome label. The row was moved from the always-loaded damaging hazard pack to a regional note at an approximate Rome city reference, **not a verified epicenter**. This does not rule out an earthquake.

Full sources, caveats, IDs and before/after images are preserved in the manifests. Selected sources: [Tegyra revision](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Battle_of_Tegyra&oldid=1370275344), [Cynoscephalae revision](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Battle_of_Cynoscephalae_(364_BC)&oldid=1370438027), [Suessula revision](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Battle_of_Suessula&oldid=1378105602), [Third Sacred War revision](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Third_Sacred_War&oldid=1370750563), [Helike revision](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Helike&oldid=1364714256), [Delphi study](https://storicamente.org/broeck_temple_apollo_delphi), [NOAA event 21](https://www.ngdc.noaa.gov/hazel/hazard-service/api/v1/earthquakes/21), and [Guiling revision](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Battle_of_Guiling&oldid=1370442266). The academic interpretations cited for Helike do not all agree; neither establishes every feature of the disaster.

## Verification and rollback

Fourteen context and twelve hazard tests passed before and after the relevant applications. Twenty-one exact installed after-images and five pack objects were logically rolled back and reapplied in memory, with hazards reversed before context. There was no actual rollback write. Writes are atomic **per pack**, not across packs. To reverse, run `apply_389_340_bce_hazards.py rollback` before `apply_389_340_bce_context.py rollback`, and reverse this batch before older installers affecting the same packs. Guard checks and later batch dependencies must be considered first.

No live Minecraft, loader, save, full integrity test, recent Java probe or rebuilt jar was run; the jar remains stale. Natural VIP spawning and alternate-history behavior remain unimplemented.
