Its c.2450 slot is only a reference; [Eannatum](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Eannatum&oldid=1381348313) and the [stele](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Stele_of_the_Vultures&oldid=1377780794) articles give different approximate estimates. This is not an exact dated battle, an established physical meeting at the findspot, or proof that all named persons appear on the stele. Ancient Lagash inscriptions express its perspective.

Existing IDs are retained where possible. Both notes are indexed as cultural notes, but source evidence is not necessarily shown by all readers. NewsIndex can repeat interval notes and may include the stored end year. No live loader was checked in this batch.

## Connected-people research

`history/sources/wikipedia_2489_2440_people_links.json` contains **28 person/name references and 38 qualified relationship assertions**. They include Mesilim, Lugalshaengur, Ur-Nanshe of Lagash, Akurgal, Eannatum, Enannatum I, Enmetena, Pabilgagaltuku, Ush, Enakalle, Ur-Lumma, Bara-irnun and Gishakidu; and the Fifth Dynasty family and officials including Sahure, Meretnebty, Neferirkare, Khentkaus II, Neferefre, Nyuserre and Washptah. Local research IDs cover four named officials/princes without a verified personal Wikidata item in this pass. No births, deaths or exact dates are inferred. Several people and the source inscriptions belong to different generations, some outside the batch.

The Lagash ruler Ur-Nanshe is not equated with the Mari singer Ornina/Ur-Nanshe; Neferirkare’s earlier personal name Ranefer is not the same person as his son Neferefre, also called Ranefer. Some apparent succession, kinship and victory claims are debated. These are **research-only**, not spawnable VIP profiles or implemented alternate-timeline behavior; the same people may appear in prior research files, not as new global person counts.
