## Indexed game history

Three existing records were corrected or qualified, and three context notes were added:

- **Hekla 3:** an additional record in the world-events pack had an exact-year `disaster`/`lockdown` label. It is now a note because the eruption date and its effects are debated. Previously reviewed 1100 and 1159 BCE Hekla notes may describe the same eruption under competing chronologies; they remain separate pending deduplication. This change concerns the world-events record, not a move from the disaster pack, and has not been tested in game.
- **Michmash:** changed the inherited exact-year military-presence record to a note identifying the biblical narrative. The cited Wikipedia article itself calls for critical secondary sources; the date, troop figures, battlefield and historicity of the episode are not established by that narrative alone. This is not a claim that the event is disproved.
- **Kenyan highlands:** replaced an unsupported claim that farming first appeared at the inherited point and year with a broader East African food-production context. Livestock herding and crop cultivation have different histories. A 2024 Kakapel study documents cowpea remains about 2,300 years old; this is not proof of a single first-farming date for the region.
- **Assyrian succession:** added a note for Shalmaneser II and his son Ashur-nirari IV, with the later succession by his uncle Ashur-rabi II. The precise dates and circumstances are uncertain; missing names in an eponym list do not prove a violent overthrow.
- **Uzzah and the Ark:** added a clearly labelled biblical-tradition note, not proof of an exact 1010 BCE death, location or supernatural cause.
- **Ugarit tablet:** added a note distinguishing the proposed 1012 BCE eclipse identification from other proposed dates and from actual observation. Scholars also question whether the text describes an eclipse. The decade page's minute-level claim is not treated as established; Ugarit's destruction is generally dated to the early 12th century BCE.

The mapped points and one-year slots are references, not proof of exact ancient places or dates. An inclusive indexed-hazard-pack scan found zero overlapping stored intervals, but the separate Hekla world-event record shows why that is not a complete disaster audit. No conclusion about the absence of real disasters follows.
