# 800–760 BCE: partial research and game-data changes

**Focus years:** 800, 790, 780, 770 and 760 BCE. These are ten-year steps; the years between them remain for later.

**Overall progress: 234 focus years partly worked; 0 fully collected.** Selected sources were researched, but not every linked article or event has been collected.

## Changes installed in the game

The indexed game JSON now has **35 corrected records**, **25 moved records** and **6 new context notes**. The moves comprise 24 uncertain-date volcanoes and an uncertain Helos battle record. Existing IDs were kept. The new notes identify broad contexts; they do not invent a personal action in the slot year.

- **800 BCE:** The Greek period boundary and the Olmec/La Venta, Paracas and Etruscan records are now qualified as broad archaeological context. The decade’s dating of the Greek epics, British Iron Age, sundial and steel-use claims needs caution. The Heraion Teichos medicine-area story does not establish a pharmaceutical facility at 800 BCE.
- **790 BCE:** The Assyrian southern campaign’s target and route are uncertain. Alara is recorded as a Kushite reign-context lead, with the decade’s proposed years distinguished from the biography’s broader eighth-century date.
- **780 BCE:** Chinese eclipse identifications are disputed; the 780 and 781 claims cannot both be treated as certain first observations. Erebuni is presented as a fortress conventionally founded in 782, not a new founding event in 780.
- **770 BCE:** The Eastern Zhou transition is commonly placed around 770, but another interpretation of the Xinian manuscript proposes a later relocation. Two erroneous pins in Gansu were changed to the Luoyang area. A separate note records the reported 770 Assyrian campaign against Marad without assuming a route or game damage.
- **760 BCE:** Ashur-dan III’s reign and Amos’s prophetic tradition are contextual notes; neither proves personal presence or an action in this exact year.

Nearby corrections included moving the 776 BCE Olympic tradition from a mistaken 779 slot to Olympia, and changing a Pārśvanātha traditional death-site reference from London to Shikharji in Jharkhand. The year and traditional details remain uncertain. The Assyrian eclipse is widely identified with 763 BCE, not 760. The 771 Western Zhou records refer to the Haojing area near Xi’an; their related notes can repeat.
