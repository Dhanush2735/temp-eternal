## Four reversible active-gameplay corrections

The changes preserve IDs, start/end, kind and effect. Location radii are gameplay visibility choices, not historical footprints. All are in `events_wikipedia_regional.json.gz` and carry pinned source evidence.

- `wpr_4316b31a87`, 1966 XB-70/F-104 collision: replaced erroneous Washington, DC with Barstow, California, as a **regional proxy**. The aircraft crashed north of Barstow; the city's point is not the collision or crash position. The 60 km radius is not an asserted impact area. [XB-70 article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_XB-70_Valkyrie#Mid-air_collision); [Barstow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barstow,_California).
- `wpr_6210741a92`, 1967 Apollo 4: launch point changed from Washington to Kennedy Space Center's LC-39A; this is not an orbital track. [Apollo 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_4); [Launch Complex 39](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Space_Center_Launch_Complex_39).
- `wpr_8b22652adb`, 1967 European Cup final: moved the record from eastern England to Estádio Nacional near Lisbon; removed the generalized player-birthplace-distance assertion rather than retaining unverified precision. [Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_European_Cup_final); [stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_Nacional); [Lisbon Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon_Lions).
- `wpr_eb98ba54a1`, 1968 Apollo 7: launch moved from Washington to Cape Kennedy LC-34; the wording no longer asserts the first live television broadcast from orbit. Apollo 7's article describes the first live TV broadcast aboard a *crewed American spacecraft*. The point is the launch site, not the entire mission. [Apollo 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_7); [LC-34](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Canaveral_Launch_Complex_34).
