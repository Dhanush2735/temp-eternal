# 1451, 1461, 1471, 1481 and 1491 CE — partial collection

**Progress: 459 years partly worked; 0 fully collected.** These five ten-year steps are not complete histories. Intervening years and many linked articles remain to be researched.

## Implemented in indexed game JSON

- **36 existing records updated and two added** in six indexed history packs. The additions cover Bahlul Lodi's accession in Delhi (1451) and Mahmud Gawan's execution at Bidar (1481).
- The 1461 Salmeniko and Trebizond records now point to Greece and Trabzon rather than Istanbul. Their annual duplicate descriptions were reduced to notes; the separately indexed sieges retain their military-presence entries. Trebizond's world siege range was corrected from 1460–1461 to 1461–1462.
- The Champa campaign is associated with Vijaya, rather than Hanoi or an unrelated point, without stating that Cham communities disappeared. The traditionally reported Inca–Mapuche Battle of the Maule remains a **disputed-date, disputed-location note**, not a certain 1471 battle.
- The papal bull *Aeterni regis* now describes the papal confirmation of the substance of Alcáçovas. The Aztec Sun Stone's old 1481 entry is a correction note: its article dates the stone to the early 1500s.
- João I of Kongo's baptism is placed at a Kongo-capital proxy rather than Lisbon. The Kongo church entry now describes construction beginning in 1491; it does not claim it was the first Christian church in sub-Saharan Africa or already a cathedral.
- The Treaty of Granada is a treaty note distinct from the siege; surrender occurred in 1492. The 1491 Peace of Pressburg points to Bratislava. A 1482 Westbroek battle record was corrected to 1481 without creating another battle ID.
- The 1491 Mahuika impact/tsunami hypothesis is recorded as disputed, rather than asserted as a disaster. A 2017 survey did not find the proposed crater-like structure. This correction applies to the specified regional row, **not a claim that every disaster or other record was reviewed**.
- The Holy Child of La Guardia entry identifies a blood-libel case, not proven murder. The 1451 Mamluk embassy report is qualified: the year page calls Mehmed II a Mamluk sultan, while Jaqmaq's article places him on the Mamluk throne. The embassy's precise details still require checking.

Other fixes include city or regional proxies for Bordeaux, Naumburg, Tangier, Zahara, Westminster, Cork, Langeais and the Utrecht conflict. A proxy is an approximate reference point, not a proven precise event site.
