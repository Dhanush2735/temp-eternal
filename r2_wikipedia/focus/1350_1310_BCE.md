# 1350–1310 BCE: partial history collection

**Years worked:** 1350, 1340, 1330, 1320 and 1310 BCE. These are five ten-year steps, not all years in between. They are not fully collected.

## What changed in the game

Seven existing history records were corrected, two epidemic records moved out of the disaster pack into historical notes, and two regional context notes added. These are changes to indexed game resources, not just a research document. Original IDs were preserved for corrections and moves.

- **Yinxu:** the decade overview proposes a 1350 BCE capital date, while the linked archaeological article dates the Late Shang capital period roughly 1250–1046 BCE. The retained 1350 slot is not a proven date of a move.
- **Mycenae:** two possible overlapping palace/wall records have uncertain building dates. Perseus and Andromeda are legendary figures, not verified builders or rulers; the political effect was changed to a note. Both records can still appear.
- **San Lorenzo:** the Bajío phase is a long period, not proof of a building event in 1350. **Athens:** 1340 is an approximate period boundary, not a proven construction date for a palace.
- **Uluburun:** 1320 is an approximate reference for the wreck. Radiocarbon and tree-ring interpretations differ; an exact sinking year is not established.
- **Egypt:** the Amenhotep III–Akhenaten transition is disputed across chronologies. Added a 1330 Tutankhamun reign-context reference; it does not assert that he was in Thebes then or that a particular event occurred that year.
- **Hatti:** added a 1310 Muršili II reign-context reference. The king's accession dates and a possible eclipse correlation are debated; no eclipse is asserted in 1310 and the map point does not prove his personal location.
- **Megiddo and Hittite disease:** the Amarna letters report pestilence at Megiddo and Hittite prayers describe an epidemic. Exact timing, pathogen, geographical extent and any connection between them are uncertain. Removed the two entries from the disaster loader and retained them as cautious regional notes; Hattusa replaces an unsupported Egypt-centered Hittite reference. This does not prove the epidemics were unrelated. IDs remain the same, but saved-world effects have not been tested.
