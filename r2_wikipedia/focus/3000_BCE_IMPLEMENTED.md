# 3000 BCE — gameplay data updated

## Latest: Morocco and Sumer

- **Oued Beht, Morocco:** added a local game note about Neolithic farming. Archaeological dates span roughly 3400–2900 BCE, so 3000 is an approximate reference, not a proven exact year. The site was found while researching North African agriculture; it is an additional related source, not yet a confirmed link in the focused Wikipedia chain.
- **Oued Beht site record:** removed an unsupported Roman civilization label. Other game systems may still choose a civilization. The existing broad dates and `town` rank remain unreviewed and do not prove uninterrupted occupation or a permanent town.
- **Sumer:** corrected a claim that cities were established in exactly 3000 BCE. Cities including Uruk existed earlier; this is now a regional cultural note.

Sources: [Oued Beht](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Oued_Beht_(archaeological_site)&oldid=1382237474), [archaeological study](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2024.101), [later study](https://doi.org/10.1017/lis.2026.10037), [Uruk](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Uruk&oldid=1380485388), and [the Met's Uruk essay](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/uruk-the-first-city). Before/after records and rollback information: `reviews/3000_morocco_sumer_2026-10.json`.

## Previous: three site records corrected

The game also uses separate site records to identify settlements. Three had incorrect civilization labels:

- **Shahr-e Sukhteh, Iran:** removed the Sumerian label and revised its broad occupation window using the differing Wikipedia and UNESCO estimates.
- **Caral, Peru:** removed an Inca placeholder from this much earlier site. Its existing game dates were retained.
- **Neolithic Knossos, Crete:** removed the Sumerian label. This record ends before the game's 3000 BCE starting limit.

The civilization field is now blank rather than replaced with a guess. The game may still use other map or ruler data when choosing an occupant; existing saved characters were not changed. Source notes and original records are in `reviews/early_site_attribution_2026-10.json`.

## Tests and remaining work

The full Python suite passed 123 tests, with unclosed-test-database warnings. Headless tests loaded the staged and installed data through the real Gazetteer, NewsIndex, CivHints and CivSynthesis classes, using stubs for other dependencies. They checked the site records and that a blank civilization hint does not become a synthesized civilization. The corrected events no longer appear at the old Tara, Colombia and wheat locations; regional entries do not appear as local pins. This is not a full Forge build or Minecraft gameplay test.

The new event entries are historical notes, not new civilization or villager-spawning definitions. The four site-label corrections above remove specific wrong hints, but do not prevent every possible wrong assignment from other game data.

Other 3000 BCE records are not yet fully reviewed. The Prometheus tree has two overlapping estimated-germination entries, and shekel and North African agriculture claims need further review. Oued Beht’s inherited site interval and town rank also need a separate model-aware review; later periods at the site are documented, not continuous occupation. The overview's c. 3000 BCE potter's-wheel claim also needs review: [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/a-salute-to-the-wheel-31805121/) describes wheels used for pottery around 3500 BCE. Another overlapping Knossos site entry still needs its chronology reviewed. Other existing entries may overlap or have uncertain dating. **3000 BCE is not complete; years completed: 0.**
