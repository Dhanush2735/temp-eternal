## Indexed game JSON

**Twelve existing records were corrected or qualified, one contextual note added, and ten volcano records moved from the active hazard pack to indexed historical notes.** IDs and nominal slots remain; possible duplicates are not silently merged. The historic events can still produce game notes. Changes are in the project resources, not just research files; an updated game jar has not been built or tested.

- **Syria:** corrected a third Ugarit record from a Palestine-area point to Ras Shamra. [Ugarit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugarit) gives 35.602° N, 35.782° E and describes Late Bronze flourishing over centuries, not a single-year peak. Two related previously qualified records may repeat it.
- **Egypt and Mitanni:** added a non-political diplomacy reference at c. 1400 BCE. The 1420s page places Thutmose IV's treaty in that decade despite his [linked reign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thutmose_IV) of c. 1401–1391 BCE. [Artatama I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artatama_I) describes a diplomatic marriage, but conflicts with [Mutemwiya's biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutemwiya), which rejects her identification as the Mitanni princess. The new note asserts no exact signing date, location, or certain identification. Enriched earlier Amenhotep II and III notes with succession and family context.
- **Anatolia:** the 1390s overview's claim that Arnuwanda I ruled until 1360 conflicts with [his biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnuwanda_I), which proposes c. 1390–1380. Replaced the fixed political effect with a qualified note mentioning Queen Ašmu-Nikkal and [Tudhaliya II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudhaliya_II); numbering and co-rule are debated. The long Kaska–Hittite conflict record is now a qualified broad reference, not proof of uninterrupted war at an exact frontier.
- **Greece and Crete:** qualified three inherited Mycenaean/Crete records and a combined palaces/Linear B note. [Mycenaean Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_Greece) puts the civilization's beginning earlier than c. 1400; palaces, scripts and cultural shifts developed in different places and phases. The notes do not establish a single transition year.
- **Hattusa:** a Lion Gate date is not fixed at 1400 BCE. [Hattusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hattusa) describes the fortified outer city; [a separate overview](https://www.ancient-history-sites.com/sites/lion-gate-hattusa/) reports competing later dates and a late-14th-century proposal. The relevant excavation publications were not independently inspected. The old slot is a reference, not a construction date.
- **South Asia:** converted the long Ten Kings war record to a qualified Rigvedic tradition note. [The battle article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Ten_Kings) describes Sudas, priests and opposing groups and proposes a 14th-century date, but the exact date, place, details and interpretations are uncertain; the stored range is not a verified battle duration. Also qualified an inherited Assyrian power-ranking claim.

Coordinates and radii are reference locations, not established battlefields, territories or dates. Other inherited and overlapping events remain unreviewed.
