# Preliminary batch: 12–61 CE

**Status: partly worked; no year fully collected.** This covers 50 consecutive historical years. Earlier work had already sampled 21, 31, 41, 51 and 61 CE, so adding 50 to the previous partly-worked total would double-count those years. The old shared-endpoint total of 3,168 remains unreconciled; no replacement aggregate of partly worked years is asserted without a year-by-year coverage audit.

## Wikipedia and coverage inventory

- All 50 chronological year titles resolved; all redirected. The 48 titles for 12–59 CE redirect to five decade pages, while 60 and 61 resolve to `AD 60` and `AD 61`. Seven distinct canonical revisions are archived in `year_wikitext_12_61_CE.json.gz`; raw wikitext leaves transclusions unexpanded. The six relevant decade pages and selected linked articles were only partly consulted. Title resolution alone is not an annual history or evidence that all linked material was read.
- `audit_title_inventory.py` independently counts **3,050 distinct chronological titles** resolved in the existing title-result files: 2,989 BCE titles (2989–1 BCE) and 61 CE titles (1–61 CE). It explicitly excludes the exploratory `AD 1` disambiguation-title set. It does not find individual title-result records for 3000–2990 BCE; that is a file-inventory observation, not a claim those years were never researched. This count is **not** the number of fully collected or even fully reviewed years.
- A scan found **277 indexed rows starting in 12–61 CE**, including 73 hazard rows. Earlier-starting overlaps were excluded and most candidates were not individually reviewed. The scan is retained as `indexed_start_scan_12_61_CE.txt.gz`.

## Indexed game-data corrections

Fifteen guarded updates were applied to existing rows across three indexed packs. They retain IDs; contextual notes do not assert that the underlying events were unreal.

- The **Siege of Brundisium** was moved from 49 CE to 49 BCE. Wikidata's entry has a 49 CE date but identifies Caesar's Civil War; its Dutch sitelink and the corresponding English article describe the 49 BCE siege. It is a note until duplicate effects are reconciled. Likewise, the Rimini Arch of Augustus was moved from 20 CE to 27 BCE and identified as a gate; its article says the triumphal-arch designation is disputed.
- The game ID `q29056780` is the **43 CE British Thames event**, not the better-known **1813 Canadian Battle of the Thames**. Its Wikidata coordinates conflict and lack references; it remains a 43 CE non-military regional note rather than being mistakenly moved across centuries and continents.
- Kunyang's broad war record was moved from 17 to **23 CE** and made duplicate context; the separately indexed battle remains active. The Korean War note was moved from 25 CE to **1950–1953**, ending with the armistice, not a peace treaty.
- Castra Praetoria was dated to 23; the Gengshi proclamation to 23, with its article's different accession milestones distinguished; Guangwu's proclamation and Luoyang capital to 25; the Alexandrian attacks to 38; Herod Antipas's exile to 39; Guo Shengtong's replacement by Yin Lihua to 41; and Ma Yuan's campaign against the Trưng rebellion to 42–43. These are selective corrections, not proof that every action or coordinate is fully established. The rebellion as a whole began in 40; competing accounts of the sisters' deaths remain.
- The 17 CE Lydia earthquake's extra world-pack lockdown was converted to context because the separate NOAA-derived hazard already exists and epicenter estimates differ. The 37 CE Antioch annual disruption is now context for the separately indexed earthquake; NOAA's account discusses later historical evidence and an inferred cause. Other active effects and damage radii were not validated in a live world.

## Hazards

Ten hazard records moved from the damaging hazards pack to the indexed regional-note pack. Eight were demonstrably modern events incorrectly assigned to early CE: two New Madrid sequence records (1811–1812); the two Lovatnet rockslides and lake waves (1905 and 1936); Nice (1979); Messina (1908); Bristol Channel flooding (1607); and Ierissos (1932). For Messina and Ierissos, earthquake deaths must not automatically be attributed to a tsunami. The Bristol Channel article favors a storm surge rather than a tsunami. These notes do not implement the modern disasters' full gameplay effects or prove there are no duplicates.

Two other rows were contextualized without claiming the event did not happen: NOAA 8178 cites a scholarly assessment questioning the Jerusalem earthquake tradition's value as a historical earthquake record, while NOAA 46 gives archaeological Ai-Khanoum damage possibly dating anywhere from **50 BCE to 50 CE**, alongside an exact 50 CE source. The latter note remains anchored at 50 CE as an unresolved endpoint; its title and review note explicitly state the broader uncertainty. A note about a historical claim is not an evaluation of religious beliefs.

At least **63 of the 73 scanned hazard rows were not moved by this batch**, and many remain active and unreviewed. NOAA reports additional ancient earthquakes. Smithsonian GVP lists a confirmed Santorini eruption around **46–47 CE**, with dating uncertainty; the matching eruption/tsunami rows were not moved or live-tested. Historical reporting does not establish an exact in-game uniform damaging radius.

## Connected people

`history/sources/wikipedia_12_61_ce_people_links.json` has **29 research references, 15 qualified relationships and 28 event roles over 11 indexed IDs**. It includes both sides and associated figures for Kunyang, the Trưng rebellion, Brundisium and the Boudican revolt, as well as connected Han, Roman and Herodian figures. Several people have only an event-article mention rather than a separate verified biography; the two Trưng sisters share their joint biography link but have separate IDs. Named roles, relationships, dates and physical presence still need independent checking. None are spawnable VIPs.

## Validation, rollback and limitations

- Context manifest canonical SHA-256 `633d4abe5894552fb8f30ed9e5ca2349995d195be1caa2885249daf9218b2f39`; hazard moves `762be469945eba7ea3e85915c55852b233b7004223ac4609c0c03eac446cb022`.
- Both installers were staged before application. **10 Python tests passed after application**, verifying hashes, unique indexed after-images, research references and dependency-ordered logical reversal/reapplication of affected pack objects in memory. Actual rollback writes and saved worlds were not tested. Writes are atomic per pack, not transactional across packs. Reverse this batch's hazard moves before its context updates, and then reverse older shared-pack installers only in dependency order.
- World/Wikidata and war-pack NewsIndex can display an exclusive end as an additional inclusive year; Wikipedia regional packs are handled differently. The 1 BCE/1 CE clock's internal year-zero problem remains unresolved. Coordinates and radii often remain regional proxies.
- No live game, loader, save, full integrity check, Java probe or jar rebuild was performed. The jar is stale. Natural VIP spawning, historically guided NPC behavior and player-triggered branching are not implemented.
