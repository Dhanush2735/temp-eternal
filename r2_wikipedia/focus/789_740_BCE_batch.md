# 789–740 BCE — preliminary R2 batch

**Status: partly worked, not complete.** The 50 year-title checks resolved to five decade pages rather than 50 independent histories. Selected linked articles and specific records were reviewed; this is not exhaustive worldwide collection or a complete traversal of all links. With five previously counted step endpoints, this batch adds 45 to the existing shared-period count: **2,538 years partly worked; 0 fully collected**. This count does not imply that each year has a complete or independently sourced history. Next chronological batch: 739–690 BCE; earlier years remain incomplete.

## Active indexed changes

Thirteen existing rows were updated using stable IDs and four questionable disaster rows were moved from the active hazards pack to the indexed regional note pack. All retain their original before-images and sourced after-images in guarded rollback manifests. The intent is to replace unsupported deterministic gameplay effects with qualified event/news context; the affected rows may still appear in the Gazetteer or NewsIndex. Their note status is not a guarantee of identical user-visible behavior across all readers. The `wikipedia_region` confidence value is used for the engine's regional handling; explicit GVP/NOAA source evidence remains attached and should not be read as a claim that those sources are Wikipedia.

- Olympic context distinguishes Coroebus as the **first recorded** victor in later tradition, not proof of the first competition ever. Earlier attached evidence was preserved.
- Cyzicus's 756 BCE Milesian-colony claim is qualified: the city article records earlier traditions and an external encyclopedia reports 679 as an alternative and says pottery favors the later date. Trabzon's date is a source-based claim rather than a proven exact political transition.
- The Ashur-nirari V succession and the 746/745 revolt and Tiglath-Pileser III succession are qualified; a coup is not assumed. Two Menahem/ Shallum chronology estimates are marked as possible duplicate accounts of one succession. Kashta–Piye dating, Meles's accession and Roman triumph/Titus Tatius stories are explicitly uncertain or traditional.
- `q103201061` is now a disputed siege note, rather than military presence. Wikidata lists Arpad at 743 without a reference; the decade page gives capture in 740. A 743 battle near Arpad is not automatically the same siege. Another row may duplicate it.
- `q3142923` retained its ID but moved from a 743 Arpad point to a **714 BCE reference** at a broad Van-area point and became a note. The linked Wikipedia account describes Sargon II's 714 campaign; the 743 battle is separate. Wikidata's own start/end and part-of claims conflict. Existing `wpw_7518498873` may duplicate this account. This date/coordinate change can affect saved-world news and suppressions; the reference point is not the battle route.
- Three GVP eruptions are confirmed but not known to have occurred in the pack's exact reference year or caused the modeled one-year disruption: Nemrut Dagi 787 BCE ±25 years, Porak 778 BCE ±5, and Kie Matubu 749 BCE ±77. The NOAA earthquake record's quoted source says date, location and magnitude cannot presently be assessed and reports no direct or indirect evidence of damage to Jerusalem. Moving the four entries out of `events_hazards.json.gz` matters because `Disasters.java` reads every row in that pack, even if its kind/effect is changed. Their IDs, former fields and approximate reference dates remain recoverable; these are notes, not evidence that the events never happened. Existing radii and one-year slots are not proven impact footprints or exact dates.

The indexed overlap scan identified 84 rows in the range, not 84 verified distinct events. Only the selected rows above were reviewed for this change; other possible duplicates, incorrect locations, missing people and unsupported claims remain.
