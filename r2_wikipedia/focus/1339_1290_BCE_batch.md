## Indexed gameplay resources

**18 existing records corrected or qualified; 11 uncertain-date volcano records moved from the destructive hazard pack to indexed notes.** IDs and inherited lookup slots remain stable, but the dates are not established by those slots. Changes are in project resources; no updated jar or game test was performed.

- **Anatolia and Syria:** [Aleppo Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleppo_Treaty) was incorrectly mapped to London. The [British Museum](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_1989-0128-1) identifies its tablet's findspot as Bogazköy and describes a reissue by Muwatalli II of a Muršili II–Talmi-Šarruma agreement. Hattusa is now a findspot reference, **not** a demonstrated signing location; the one-year slot is uncertain. The [Muršili solar sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mursili%27s_eclipse) reference point was moved from southern Italy to Hattusa as an archive reference, not an observation point or Hayasa-Azzi campaign location. Eclipse identification and date are debated. Arinnanda's existing Ephesus point is now explicitly a western Anatolian reference, not a battlefield; [researchers have proposed different locations](https://www.academia.edu/41502855/The_Last_Foothold_of_Arzawa_The_Location_of_Puranda_and_Mount_Arinnanda_Revisited_in_Kim_R_I_Myn%C3%A1%C5%99ov%C3%A1_J_Pav%C3%BAk_P_eds_Hrozn%C3%BD_and_Hittite_The_First_Hundred_Years_Leiden_Boston_544_570).
- **Egypt:** Qualified [Smenkhkare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smenkhkare), Tutankhamun's accession, name change, death and burial, Ay and Horemheb succession, and [Ramesses I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_I) with competing chronologies. Two 1306 Horemheb rows may duplicate each other; the 1323 succession and 1325 burial references can describe the same death. IDs remain for later reconciliation. The [Zannanza affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zannanza) is no longer presented as the certain assassination of Ankhesenamun's intended husband: the Egyptian widow's identity, death circumstances and location are disputed. [Karnak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnak) is not newly constructed in 1295; its Amun precinct developed over centuries, beginning earlier.
- **Assyria:** The 1310s decade places Enlil-nirari's accession in 1317; [his biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlil-nirari) gives c. 1327–1318 and [Arik-den-ili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arik-den-ili) c. 1317–1306. The inherited 1319 slot is now a succession-context lookup, explicitly identifying the mismatch. [Adad-nirari I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adad-nirari_I) has alternative chronologies; an inherited point was corrected to Assur as a reference, not a proven coronation site.
- **Mediterranean:** Qualified the late 14th-century [Uluburun shipwreck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uluburun_shipwreck) date. Its separately indexed 1320 and 1295 records may overlap; they were not silently merged.
- **Source disagreement:** The 1310s page attributes the Bhagavad Gita to this decade according to tradition, whereas the linked [article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhagavad_Gita) discusses much later scholarly composition dates. No unsupported ancient composition event was added.

Coordinates and radii are spatial lookup references, not proof of presence, event footprints or exact locations. Other inherited records remain unreviewed.
