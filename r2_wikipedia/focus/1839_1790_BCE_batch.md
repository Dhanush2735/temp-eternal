## Indexed game JSON

Four existing notes were updated; no new event was added:

- The Rim-Sîn I of Larsa note now includes qualifications about [Kudur-Mabuk, Warad-Sin and En-ane-du](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kudur-Mabuk). One inscription's wording differs on kinship, and a proposed identification of En-ane-du with Manzi-wartaš is not assumed. A Larsa governor named Sîn-muballiṭ is not automatically identified with Babylon's king of the same name.
- The Shamshi-Adad I note records family and the qualified [Eshnunna Naram-Sin connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naram-Sin_of_Eshnunna); attribution of the exile narrative and dates vary. Eshnunna's Naram-Sin is distinct from the rulers of Assur and Akkad bearing the same name.
- The [Kahun papyrus note](https://collections.ucl.ac.uk/Details/petrie/40186) now references the Petrie Museum catalogue, the Lahun find location and women's health content. Its treatments are historical, not modern medical advice; its date is approximate.
- A generic Eshnunna war-pack note had no named conflict. Its map reference was corrected from 32.395 N, 47.5542 E to [Tell Asmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eshnunna), 33.48417 N, 44.72833 E; it is labeled uncertain regional context, not a claim that war continued across the inherited date range or that the point is a battle location. The old ID, dates and radius are retained. It remains a note in the war pack; its particular original source is not established.

Eighteen individually matched, imprecisely dated volcano entries were moved from the destructive hazard pack into indexed non-disruptive notes. Profile links and dating methods are recorded in each new note and the hazard manifest. A question mark does not imply a numerical range; Reclus's 'in or after' is not a symmetric interval. These changes do not deny eruptions or damage. Existing reference slots, map points and radii are not exact dates or measured footprints.

The old **Chile tsunami** entry remains in the hazard pack, unchanged. [A 2022 study](https://www.science.org/doi/full/10.1126/sciadv.abm2996) proposes a large northern Chile earthquake and tsunami around 3,800 calendar years before present and a modeled magnitude near 9.5. The old entry's particular source and identity have not been established well enough to equate the two records or safely move it. A NOAA volcano API search for nominal −1839 to −1790 returned no items; that does not establish absence from NOAA.
