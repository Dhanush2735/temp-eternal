# 450–410 BCE: five partly worked years

**Years:** 450, 440, 430, 420 and 410 BCE. These are ten-year steps; the intervening years were not separately completed. This is partial research, not a complete collection of the Wikipedia link chain or all worldwide history.

## What was implemented

Changed the indexed game history JSON: **25 updated records, 21 records moved, and one new record**. IDs were preserved for changed and moved records. The manifest records exact before- and after-images and sources: `reviews/450_410_bce_context_2026-10.json.gz` (canonical SHA-256 `f0df372bee14199015ae2a7f5047bbc3354fbbe4efaafbe503b87cec1d7c9dd8`). `apply_450_410_context.py` provides guarded staging, application and rollback. Writes are atomic per file, not across files; rollback is guarded against later edits.

- Corrected the active Battle of Salamis **in Cyprus** from the other Salamis near Athens, retaining its military effect. Corrected active military records for Potidaea (432 BCE), Amphipolis (422 BCE), the initial Theban attack on Plataea (431 BCE), the later siege of Plataea (429–427 BCE) and the Samian War (440–439 BCE). Different parts of the Plataea article disagree about the end date; the attack and later siege are separate. A duplicate Potidaea entry is now a note, not a second military effect. The related Salamis campaign entry remains a note.
- **The plague of Athens remains an active disaster.** Its hazard was wrongly centered in Libya; it is now centered on Athens, with its existing plague/lockdown classification preserved. The 25-km game radius is an approximation, not a measured ancient boundary, and the game uses estimated severity. The disease's cause remains unknown. Duplicate plague records were changed to notes rather than asserting epidemic typhus or adding duplicate active effects. Repeated outbreak waves are modeled as one continuous interval; this is a simplification.
- The disaster loader treats its end year as inclusive, so the active plague hazard uses 430–426 BCE. The Gazetteer treats the stored end as exclusive, so its pin does not include 426 BCE; the duplicate regional note includes 426. These systems' differing date semantics are a known limitation, not proof of exact epidemic duration.
- Fifteen volcano hazards were moved to indexed regional notes: the Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program lists eruption evidence but assigns uncertain dates and unknown end dates. The old exact-year damage effects could not be justified. Existing history and IDs remain available as notes; the moves may affect saved-world behavior.
- Corrected or qualified several cultural and political entries: Zhou capital-region references, Perdiccas II's disputed accession date, the Empedocles philosophy entry, the Doryphoros, the Riace bronzes, the Berlin Foundry Cup, the old Athenian Bouleuterion, the Temple of Athena Nike and the disputed Peace of Callias. An artwork's modern museum or discovery site is not proof of where it was made. The traditional Roman famine and selected ruler political effects remain active with uncertainty noted.
- Added a **regional historical note** for Cerro del Gentil and Paracas, Peru, around 450 BCE. The article dates the ceremonial site approximately 550–200 BCE; 450 is a reference point, not a known construction date. The coordinate is a Chincha Alta regional reference, not the site's exact coordinate. This note does not create a structure or a new gameplay mechanic.

## Future VIP research

Ten research-only people leads were saved in `eternal/src/main/resources/data/eternalengine/eternal/history/sources/wikipedia_450_410_bce.json`, including Cimon, Cynisca, Ducetius, King Kao and Evagoras I. They do not create spawnable VIPs. The already-existing Pericles VIP was flagged for later review, not validated here. Historical schedules, on-demand VIP spawning, and player-caused alternate-history behavior have not been implemented by this batch.

## Checks and limits

- Eleven guarded-installer tests passed before and after application, including rollback round trips, changed-record and duplicate rejection, and preservation of the plague's active kind and dates.
- Staged and installed headless Java probes passed for all 47 records in `HistoryGazetteer`, `NewsIndex` and `Disasters`; they checked the plague at Athens, its radius, inclusive dates and available damage mechanics. The previous batch's 44-record headless probe also passed. All 47 installed after-images and indexed ID locations were checked.
- A focus-year inclusive scan of hazard, war, world, more-Wikidata and nature packs found the intended active Athens plague plus two more-Wikidata military rows requiring review: Battle of Motya `q9173290` and the broad Peloponnesian War `q33745`. Seven war-pack notes overlap one or more focus years. Other packs and related dates can contain duplicates. In particular, the 411 BCE Cynossema regional entry `wpr_45f2ffec9f` still needs correction of its location and account of Alcibiades; it was not modified in this batch.
- Only selected linked pages and supporting GVP profiles were consulted; the crawler remains blocked by the storage guard. Sources are current URLs, not independently revision-pinned. No full Forge/Minecraft or saved-world test was run, so these headless checks do not establish that all in-game behavior works. The wider game review remains for after collection and implementation.
