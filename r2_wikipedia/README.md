## Current focus: 3rd millennium BCE

The current priority is the chain starting at `3rd millennium BC`, covering 3000–2001 BCE. Run bounded batches with `python r2_wikipedia/crawl.py --scope third-millennium-bc --max-pages 3` and `python r2_wikipedia/people.py --scope third-millennium-bc --max-check 3 --batch-size 1`. `millennium_scope.py` follows recorded current-revision links through already-read pages, prioritizes in-range century/decade/year titles and interleaves other linked articles. Explicitly out-of-range period titles are deferred, not discarded; ordinary linked articles can concern any era. People checks use independent scheduling and current-revision source links, including reached biographies; a linked title is not proof of a person or historical date. The prior worldwide collection and game data remain intact. The queue grows and this traversal does not prove it contains all relevant articles or all history.

Scoped collection must still obey the existing storage guards. The full-year certification system does not exist yet: checked/redirected year titles and read year articles are not completed years.

The collection starts with the user-supplied [List of decades, centuries, and millennia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_decades,_centuries,_and_millennia). The goal is to traverse its English Wikipedia article-link graph, collect material relevant to 3000 BCE–3000 CE, and eventually build tested, source-linked data for Eternal Engine. **This is not a completed history dataset or full mod replacement. Eight reviewed event-row corrections are installed in existing game packs.**

## What is implemented

- `crawl.py`: a resumable SQLite-backed collector. It saves each visited article's page ID, revision, revision time, retrieval time, compressed wikitext and namespace-0 article links. A frontier records pending, done, missing and error titles. Retries, delay, a per-run page limit, and a database-size guard are provided. Redlinks remain edges but are not queued. Redirect targets are resolved by Wikipedia.
- `periods.py`: recognizes time-period title candidates that intersect the requested range. The crawler prioritizes chronology while interleaving a non-period-title candidate on every fifth request by default, rotating through source-era bands; a persistent attempt counter makes this work across short runs. Redirects and imperfect title classification mean such attempts can still land on period pages. Source-era rotation is a scheduling aid, not proof of historical or geographic coverage. `--other-every 0` disables interleaving. The root is the only default seed; earlier example pages remain in the database.
- `extract_candidates.py`: copies bullet-level candidates from visited period pages into compressed JSON Lines. It retains the original wikitext, links, parent bullets, section headings and source revision. Periods describe the page's context, **not an asserted event date**. Numeric year titles are checked against the page's year header before extraction. A candidate is not automatically a fact and is not game-ready.
- `coverage.py`: reports page and queue counts by period title. It does not measure the proportion of all history covered.
- `audit_future_game_events.py`: scans indexed game event start/year fields against a supplied date, copying future-year starts to a separate review-only file. It does not infer dates from titles, examine non-event resources, interpret end conventions or identify events later within the current year. A future-start row may be a plan, projection or mistake, not a verified prediction.
- `vacuum_people.py`: with all collectors stopped, compacts a verified copy of the people database and compares its schema, every physical table, queue order and deterministic biography export before atomic replacement. Its backup is temporary, in excluded cache, and is not persisted. This creates only limited headroom; it does not change the storage cap.
- `progress.py`: computes and writes `STATUS.md` and `progress.json` from the current databases. The visible report shows the number of years certified complete (currently zero), distinct recognized year pages read, and other work counts rather than percentages. A year-page read is not a completed year; redirects do not count as distinct period pages. The machine-readable JSON retains some legacy calculated percentage fields for compatibility.
- `prepare_history.py`: turns every extracted bullet into a source-linked review record with its section, original wikitext, article links and period context. It flags simple leading year labels with colons or spaced dashes (using negative years for BCE and preserving `c.` approximation, while rejecting obvious year ranges), rather than guessing an occurrence date or coordinates. `history_candidates.jsonl.gz` is the complete review inventory; `history_future_context.jsonl.gz` is a separate duplicate subset for entries with an explicitly future-year context, **not** a verified list of predictions. Entries with mixed present/future context remain in the inventory. Nothing is game-ready.
- `people.py`: discovers article titles from period candidates, the crawl graph and explicit links within biographies. It checks titles in batches and retains Wikipedia wikitext and available infobox fields. A persisted source-page cursor rotates checks across batches and restarts; it is a scheduling aid, not proof of geographic or era coverage. Wikidata is separately attributed: non-deprecated `P31=Q5` confirms a human, but its absence is **unconfirmed**, not proof of a non-person. Source revisions and discovery links are preserved. The results are staging for R3, not NPCs.
- `check_people.py`: checks database integrity, source revision metadata and discovery links, and the presence of the human cross-check. It does not fact-check historical claims.
- `build_vip_candidates.py`: stages compact, source-linked person records with the available birth/death, location, family, occupation, offices and other statements. Preserves disagreements, qualifiers, ranks, calendars, precision and references. Does not create NPCs. The Wikidata JSON API's BCE years use historical numbering (e.g. `-0044` = 44 BCE); unlike RDF/SPARQL, they must not be shifted by one. See [Wikibase JSON date documentation](https://doc.wikimedia.org/Wikibase/master/php/docs_topics_json.html).
- `place_candidates.py`: finds the place identifiers mentioned in VIP records and saves Wikidata coordinate claims and revisions. It does not treat a birthplace or other place as proof of a person's location throughout their life.
- `build_timelines.py`: creates source-linked, non-spawnable timeline statements for births, deaths, residences, work locations, offices and event participation. Explicit start/end qualifiers are retained as coarse year indexes only; missing or uncertain boundaries are not invented.
- `apply_reviewed_events.py` and `reviews/event_corrections_2026-10.json`: safely stage, apply or roll back a reviewed batch of three corrections to the existing `events_wikipedia.json.gz` pack. Each correction embeds revision-pinned Wikipedia evidence and corroborating sources in the game record; the manifest keeps the exact prior record, candidate snapshot and review rationale. The updater checks the expected original row, source and exact text, and refuses stale inputs. Rollback restores the three prior records logically, not byte-for-byte gzip formatting; it does not undo the separate `NewsIndex.java` change. The temporary full-pack backup under `.cache` will not persist. The updater now also blocks records with noted unresolved duplicates and scans indexed packs for exact original or revised names at the proposed year. This cannot automatically recognize all differently worded duplicates; manual review remains necessary.
- `probe_event_runtime.sh` and `java_probe/`: headless tests compile the actual `HistoryGazetteer`, year-only `NewsIndex` and index code, using stubs for unrelated clock/date components and the exact haversine function. They test staged and actual game resources, but are **not** a full mod build or Minecraft gameplay test. Requires JDK 17 and Gson 2.10.1 via `JAVA_HOME` and `GSON_JAR`; current test copies are under excluded `.cache`.
- `reconcile_events.py`: compares staged source text with events from every existing history-index pack. Exact normalized text matches and possible start-year disagreements are review leads, not identity or error verification; existing event data is not changed. The summary includes pair counts, not a count of proven errors.
- `archive_legacy_patches.py`: reversibly archives 37 old `worldfill/java_changes*.patch` files in `worldfill/legacy_java_patches.tar.xz`, verified by `legacy_java_patches.manifest.json`. Current game sources, resources and datasets are untouched. To verify: `python r2_wikipedia/archive_legacy_patches.py verify`. To restore: `python r2_wikipedia/archive_legacy_patches.py restore`; restoration checks available workspace budget and refuses to overwrite changed files. The archive is retained.
- `reconcile_people.py`: compares candidates with the mod's existing VIP and famous-people registers using conservative full-name matching and records potential date conflicts. All matches require human or source-based review; it does not merge identities or edit game data.
- `compact_people.py`, `compact_origin_ids.py`, `compact_crawl.py`, `compact_crawl_edges.py` and `compact_origin_links.py`: losslessly reduce SQLite storage by normalizing repeated source names and candidate identifiers and removing a redundant index. Each migration verifies row counts, content fingerprints, database integrity and foreign keys. The people migrations have already been applied; `compact_crawl_edges.py` converts the graph edge table to a single composite-key store and removes an unused target-only index. It preserves all rows and checks table fingerprints, integrity and foreign keys. The current graph saved 2,674,688 bytes; `compact_origin_links.py` removes repeated source identifiers in person provenance and saved 1,015,808 bytes on the then-current people database. The view and original duplicate-key semantics remain intact; fingerprints include all source dictionaries; this is a limited gain, not a storage solution for all Wikipedia. Temporary backups exist only under excluded `.cache` and are not persistent.
- `compact_subject_queue.py`: switches the people queue to a smaller pending-only index on a temporary copy, comparing logical fingerprints, deterministic biography exports, queue order, integrity and foreign keys before atomic replacement. The temporary backup is excluded from snapshots. Stop all collectors before running any migration.
- `storage_guard.py` estimates persisted workspace size excluding snapshot-excluded directories and credentials. `sqlite_budget.py` also applies a connection-local page cap to the people database, with estimated output headroom; on SQLite-full, the current import or check transaction rolls back and the collector stops. The page cap does not bound journals, exports, other processes, crawler or place database growth. The 125 MB preflight guard is an estimate, not a guaranteed snapshot quota; single operations or files outside this cap can overshoot.
- `test_*.py`: tests date boundaries and precision, source context, person discovery, redirects, duplicate handling, biography links, uncertainty and place provenance.

A full-resource audit found that geographic/time proximity can transitively group unrelated event IDs across many decades. The current game bootstrap now uses an independent-ID sibling index, so fresh forks do not infer siblings from proximity. Genuine multi-site siblings consequently do not automatically suppress together, and existing saved over-suppression is not changed. Some different rows share a single ID and can still be suppressed together: see `reviews/DEDUPLICATION_DESIGN.md` and `reviews/duplicate_event_ids.json`. `probe_sibling_audit.sh full` and `probe_timeline_manager.sh` exercise actual classes with headless stubs, not a complete mod build or actual saved-world serialization.

The 37 archived legacy patches, collector databases, provenance and review manifests are preserved. Temporary database backups and the JDK/Gson used by probes are under excluded `.cache` and will not persist in workspace snapshots. Storage remains limited; compaction offers only incremental capacity and cannot support a complete Wikipedia crawl by itself.

## Using it

From `/home/user` (Python 3 and `requests` are needed):

```sh
python r2_wikipedia/crawl.py --stats
python r2_wikipedia/crawl.py --max-pages 10
python r2_wikipedia/crawl.py --retry-errors --max-pages 10
python r2_wikipedia/extract_candidates.py
python r2_wikipedia/coverage.py --out r2_wikipedia/coverage.json
python r2_wikipedia/prepare_history.py --as-of 2026-10-28
python r2_wikipedia/reconcile_events.py
python r2_wikipedia/audit_future_game_events.py --as-of 2026-10-28  # review-only future-year audit
python r2_wikipedia/audit_duplicate_event_ids.py  # review-only ID collisions, never renumbers
bash r2_wikipedia/probe_sibling_audit.sh full     # headless full-resource grouping audit
bash r2_wikipedia/probe_timeline_manager.sh       # actual manager with in-memory state stubs
# Already applied; use rollback only if deliberately reverting this reviewed batch.
# python r2_wikipedia/apply_reviewed_events.py rollback
# JAVA_HOME=/path/to/jdk17 GSON_JAR=/path/to/gson-2.10.1.jar bash r2_wikipedia/probe_event_runtime.sh
python r2_wikipedia/people.py --max-check 20
python r2_wikipedia/people.py --stats
python r2_wikipedia/check_people.py
python r2_wikipedia/build_vip_candidates.py
python r2_wikipedia/place_candidates.py --max-check 20
python r2_wikipedia/build_timelines.py
python r2_wikipedia/reconcile_people.py
python r2_wikipedia/progress.py
python -m unittest discover -s r2_wikipedia -p 'test_*.py' -v
```

The default delay is six seconds **between API requests**, and a page normally requires two requests. Follow Wikimedia's current API rules and configure an identifiable contact in `USER_AGENT` before extended operation. The crawler's `--max-db-mb` defaults to 12 MiB; the people collector's defaults to 8 MiB. Each stops before a new batch once its limit is reached, although one import/batch can cross the threshold. After lossless provenance compaction and new collection, the persisted workspace is approximately 121.7 MB and the people database is approximately 7.9 MiB, below but close to its 8 MiB guard. Further batches may cross the threshold before the next check; this is not sufficient storage for full-scale collection. The snapshot limit is approximately 128 MB. The 125 MB workspace guard leaves only an estimated reserve and is not a guarantee. Do not disable it or increase database limits without arranging storage. The space remains limited; monitor it and arrange suitable external storage for a large crawl rather than blindly increasing limits. No background crawl is running.

## Important limitations

- The reachable graph may be extremely large. These scripts do not prove that every reachable article has been visited, or that Wikipedia contains all history. Ordinary article links are followed, not every external, category, image or other namespace URL.
- The source article revision is recorded, but the API-rendered links can depend on templates whose versions are not frozen alongside the article. The collection is not a single globally consistent dump snapshot.
- Direct index links do not cover every individual year; deeper period and article pages must still be explored. A detected year title is only a candidate until its contents are checked. The people pipeline's raw-biography link extraction reads explicit wikilinks only; template-generated links are handled by the broader rendered-link crawler when it reaches those pages.
- The people collector's Wikidata identity cross-check is supplemental, with its own revision and claims; it is not English Wikipedia text. A person without `P31=Q5` can be missed by this initial identification strategy and remains marked unconfirmed. Relationships, birth/death precision, location and roles need proper interpretation and source verification before NPC use.
- Wikipedia's coverage and statements can be incomplete, disputed or wrong. Locations, participants, time precision and relationships must not be invented. Preserve revision links and applicable attribution/licensing when data is reused.
- Four reviewed records in `events_wikipedia.json.gz` and three in `events_wikipedia_decades.json.gz` have been corrected; the rest of the game JSON and tiles remain as before. Incorrect-year cross-pack rows remain and have not been suppressed. This is not the requested complete replacement. Before broader replacement, resolve links and locations, deduplicate against existing sources, separately label recorded/estimated/projected information, generate all dependent indexes/tiles where applicable, and run full game-level tests. Existing non-Wikipedia sources must not be silently discarded.

The duplicate-ID audit snapshot mentioned above is currently stored as `reviews/duplicate_event_ids.json.xz`; `review_snapshot_archive.py duplicate_event_ids.json` verifies and reads it. Restore needs free space and does not overwrite a newly generated original.

### Faster CE batching (2026-11-03)
`python r2_wikipedia/fast_year_triage.py START END` collects/validates consecutive year revisions, inventories indexed starts and produces a heuristic triage report without modifying gameplay. It refuses incomplete archive pairs, changed inventories and unsafe overwrites. `--titles YEAR` prints approximate links from an archived year. Link extraction is not recursive collection, and duplicate/coordinate flags require source review. One batch can cover at most 50 years and remains subject to the 125 MB storage guard. See `focus/1944_1953_fast_batch.md`.
